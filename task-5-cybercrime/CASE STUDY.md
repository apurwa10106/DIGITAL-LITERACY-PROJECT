Cyberbullying is a type of cybercrime where individuals use digital platforms such as social media, messaging apps, or online forums to harass, threaten, or embarrass someone. It often involves spreading rumors, sharing private information, or posting harmful comments.

A common scenario involves a college student who shares a post on social media. A group of classmates begins making negative comments, mocking the student, and spreading edited images. Gradually, the situation escalates as more people join in, and the victim starts receiving abusive messages privately. The anonymity of online platforms often encourages such behavior, as the offenders feel less accountable.

Cyberbullying usually targets teenagers and young adults, especially those who are active on social media. Victims may feel isolated, anxious, and stressed, which can affect their mental health, academic performance, and self-confidence.

The consequences of cyberbullying can be severe, including emotional distress, depression, and in extreme cases, withdrawal from social life. It also creates a toxic online environment.

This case highlights the importance of responsible digital behavior and the need for awareness to prevent such incidents.
